An atlas of what the model sees

Dermopsis suggests which skin condition a photo shows. Then it marks the parts of the photo that drove the answer, and tests whether those marks are true.

What happens to a photo

  1. 1

    Check it is skin

    A separate model turns away photos that are not skin, such as pets, objects or screens.

  2. 2

    Compare

    The main model scores the photo against 34 skin conditions and healthy skin.

  3. 3

    Explain

    A heatmap marks the parts of the photo that drove the answer.

  4. 4

    Check the heatmap

    We cover the highlighted areas and see whether the model becomes less sure.

  5. 5

    Discard

    The photo is held in memory only while this runs, then deleted. Nothing is stored.

When to use it, and when not to

Use Dermopsis to

  • Learn how skin conditions look and how they differ
  • See how an image model reaches an answer
  • Prepare questions before seeing a GP or pharmacist

Do not use it to

  • Decide whether to seek care
  • Rule out skin cancer or any serious condition
  • Choose or change a treatment

If a rash spreads fast with a fever, or you have swelling of the face or trouble breathing, call 999. For anything that worries you, call NHS 111 or see your GP.

Built from an MSc project, open to partners

Dermopsis began as MSc research at Sheffield Hallam University and is developed by Avotrax. We would like to work with clinicians and researchers on testing it, especially across a wider range of skin tones.
